English Communication Program

📘 ECP — Unlocking Opportunity Through Language

"Language is the bridge between who you are and who the world sees you as."

The Challenge

Refugee students graduating secondary school in Rwanda often carry extraordinary potential — and a critical barrier. English proficiency, disrupted by years of displacement and limited resources in the camps, stands between their talent and their opportunity. According to UNHCR, while 70% of refugee youth complete secondary school, only 4% access higher education. Language is a primary reason why.

Our Response

The English Communication Program delivers a rigorous, immersive English learning experience — designed specifically for refugee learners. We meet students where they are, build them to where they need to be, and give them the confidence to use English as a tool for opportunity — not just a test to pass.

"We're not just learning English. We're learning how to tell the world who we are — and that changes everything."
What ECP Covers
Speaking & Pronunciation

Real conversation practice, accent clarity, and confidence-building in both formal and informal English communication.

Reading & Comprehension

Academic reading strategies, vocabulary in context, and critical reading — the foundation for university-level study.

Writing & Grammar

From structured essays to personal statements — students learn to write with clarity, accuracy, and voice.

Listening & Response

Audio comprehension exercises and structured debate — preparing students for classrooms and boardrooms alike.

Scholarship Essay Prep

How to write for scholarship applications, university admissions, and competitive programs — with real examples from past applicants.

Vocabulary Expansion

Targeted vocabulary for academic writing, career settings, and professional communication — tailored to refugee students' goals.

iSpeakLead — Interpersonal Skills Enhancement Program (ISEP)

🤝 iSpeakLead — O'Level Readiness & Boarding Prep

"Technical knowledge opens the door. How you relate to people determines whether you stay."
Kirehe (Mahama) & Karongi (Kiziba)
O'Level — Grades 7 to 9
Operates inside refugee-affiliated secondary schools

Why It Matters

Refugee students arrive in schools and workplaces with often remarkable academic resilience — but the hidden curriculum of interpersonal skills: how to collaborate, negotiate, resolve conflict, and build professional relationships — was never taught. These gaps hold back brilliant young people in institutions that value soft skills just as much as grades.

Our Response

iSpeakLead targets promising O'Level scholars for rigorous instruction in public speaking, academic aspiration mentorship, interview preparation, peer leadership, and tutoring — preparing them to win competitive national boarding school scholarships and step beyond camp-based educational constraints into Rwanda's best schools.

"When someone becomes better for themselves — more self-aware, more empathetic, more confident — they naturally become better for everyone around them. That's what iSpeakLead builds."
iSpeakLead Core Focus Areas
Public Speaking

Structured speaking practice, debate, and presentation skills that build unshakeable confidence in front of any audience.

Interview Preparation

Mock interviews, panel practice, and coaching for national boarding school scholarship selection processes.

Peer Leadership

How to lead from within a peer group — facilitating, motivating, and taking ownership without a title.

Academic Aspiration Mentorship

One-to-one and group mentorship sessions connecting students with older scholars who have already made the journey.

Tutoring & Academic Support

Targeted academic support across core subjects to strengthen overall performance alongside the leadership curriculum.

Active Listening & Empathy

Understanding before being understood — the skill that transforms conversations, builds trust, and opens doors in every environment.

iLaunch — A'Level English Immersion & Venture Genesis

🚀 iLaunch — From Surviving to Building

"We don't prepare students to wait for an opportunity. We prepare them to create one."
A'Level — Upper Secondary (S5 & S6)
100% Market-Readiness Focus
Entrepreneurship · College Counseling · Career Entry

The Shift We're Making

Too many upper-secondary refugee students finish A'Level in passive mode — waiting to see what happens next. They have intelligence, lived experience, and a rare hunger that most students never develop. What they need is a program that meets that hunger and channels it into action — whether that's launching a business, securing a university place, or entering the workforce with confidence.

What iLaunch Does

iLaunch is an intensive immersion program combining advanced English fluency with entrepreneurial project design, professional public speaking, college counseling, and career exploration. It expands the mindset from passive survival to active innovation — so every scholar leaves with a clear next step, and the skills to take it.

"iLaunch is where we stop teaching refugee students to adapt to the world and start teaching them to shape it."
iLaunch Core Modules
Advanced English Fluency

Professional-level speaking, writing, and presentation in English — built for university applications, job interviews, and entrepreneurial pitches.

Entrepreneurial Project Design

From idea to concept — students develop real business or social enterprise proposals grounded in their communities' actual needs.

Public Speaking & Pitching

Structured practice in presenting ideas persuasively — in front of panels, classrooms, investors, and admissions committees.

College Counseling

Identifying the right universities and scholarship programs, building strong applications, and writing personal statements that stand out.

Career Exploration

Exposure to diverse professional fields, conversations with practitioners, and tools for mapping a career path that fits the student's strengths.

Market Readiness

CV building, professional digital presence, mock interviews, and the practical skills needed to enter employment or tertiary education directly.

Program UpdateJuly 2025

ECP Cohort 1 Launches — 120 Students, One English Classroom at a Time

"We're not just learning English. We're learning how to tell the world who we are."

The English Communication Program opened its first cohort at G.S. Paysannat L in Mahama — the very school that once housed our founding team. Over 120 students attended. What began as an English class became something far more powerful: a conversation about identity, aspiration, and what language makes possible.

The English Communication Program officially launched as part of Cohort 1 of the L4R Scholars Bootcamp held July 21–25, 2025 at G.S. Paysannat L, Mahama Refugee Camp. Over 120 students — nearly three times the expected number — showed up ready. What followed was five days of intensive English instruction, structured debate, reading and writing sessions, and vocabulary-building workshops.

Students were grouped by academic level on Day 2, allowing ECP sessions to target each learner where they were. Graduate-track scholars received dedicated support on scholarship essay writing and university application language — a clear, practical bridge from the classroom to the future.

"The college application process stopped being a mystery. It became a map — and for the first time, they were the ones reading it."

Sessions ran daily alongside ISEP modules, allowing students to practice what they learned in ECP — communicating clearly, writing precisely, and speaking with confidence — within the same day's interpersonal skills work. The integration was intentional and its impact immediate.

ISEP
Session RecapJuly 24, 2025

ISEP Gender Equity Seminar Sparks the Room

"When someone becomes better for themselves, they become better for everyone."

Day 4 ISEP sessions opened with guided reading on gender equality and equity — and what followed surprised everyone. Students didn't just analyze the topic — they lived it, challenged it, and began rewriting it.

The Day 4 ISEP session began with guided reading on gender equality — a topic that cuts close to home for many refugee students whose communities have experienced both displacement and deep gender inequity. What started as a structured reading exercise became a powerful, organic seminar.

Students broke into small groups and confronted hard questions: What does equality look like inside our families? In our schools? In our futures? The answers varied, surprised, challenged — and ultimately inspired. By the end of the session, several students had drafted personal commitments to lead gender-inclusive spaces in their communities.

"You could see purpose arrive on their faces. It wasn't just a lesson. It was a decision."

Light Club
Partnership EventDecember 10, 2025

L4R & UNHCR Rwanda Host Graduate School Application Webinar

Co-facilitated by Ruth Nyabuto — Rhodes Scholar and Refugee-Led Research Hub contributor — this live webinar gave refugee students a direct window into what top graduate schools actually look for in applications.

On December 10, 2025, Light4Refugee co-hosted a webinar with UNHCR Rwanda on crafting strong graduate school application documents. Facilitated by Ruth Nyabuto — an experienced Rhodes Alumni and contributor to the Refugee-Led Research Hub — the session walked refugee students through the often-intimidating world of graduate applications.

Key topics covered:

  • What graduate admissions committees actually look for
  • How to write a compelling personal statement as a refugee applicant
  • What documents are required and how to strengthen each one
  • How to leverage scholarship opportunities like IRIS and Aspire

The event was attended by students from Mahama, Kigeme, and urban refugee communities in Kigali — a testament to the growing reach of the Light Club's network.

ECP
Student MilestoneJuly 23, 2025

From Blank Page to Bold Statement — ECP Day 3 Writing Session

"Words That Move, Dreams That Speak."

Day 3 ECP writing sessions saw students draft their first personal statements — some for the very first time. The room was quiet. Then it wasn't. By the end, students were reading their work aloud to each other.

On Day 3 of the Scholars Bootcamp, ECP sessions shifted focus from foundations to application — introducing students to the personal statement, the most powerful piece of writing a scholarship applicant will ever craft. For many, this was the first time they had written about themselves with the intention of being seen.

Facilitators guided students through drafting structures, prompting reflection on their journeys, their values, and their visions for the future. Some students wrote in near silence. Others asked for help with every sentence. All of them finished with something on the page — and that was the point.

"Seeing a student read their own story aloud — often for the first time — is one of the most powerful things we have witnessed in this program."

ISEP
ReflectionJuly 25, 2025

Cohort 1 Closing: Confidence Wasn't Taught — It Was Earned

"Knowing is not enough. You must take action."

The final ISEP session of Cohort 1 closed with a student sharing exercise — and what came out of the room proved that five days of interpersonal skills work leaves a mark that doesn't wash away.

The closing ISEP session of the L4R Scholars Bootcamp Cohort 1 was structured around a simple question: What are you taking with you? What followed was a hour that nobody in the room will forget — student after student standing to share what had shifted in them over five days.

One student spoke about finally understanding the difference between being heard and being understood. Another described practicing active listening with their family that very morning. A third committed out loud to becoming a mentor themselves one day — and was met with applause.

"Confidence wasn't taught in those five days. It was uncovered — from underneath all the doubt and displacement — and it belongs entirely to them."
