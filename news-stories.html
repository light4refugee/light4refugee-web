Scholars Bootcamp Program Update June 10, 2026

Breaking Barriers: L4R Launches Its Second Scholars Bootcamp

Mahama Refugee Camp · Kirehe, Rwanda · June 2026
"No human being is unfit — they just need a space and stage to contribute to a better world." — John Kelly Rukundo, Co-Founder

Two Years. Real Numbers.

When Light4Refugee held its very first bootcamp at G.S. Paysannat L in July 2025, 120 students showed up — nearly three times what the team expected. Today, less than twelve months later, L4R has officially opened applications for its second annual Scholars Bootcamp, and the organization it has become looks nothing like the one that walked nervously into those classrooms on Day 1.

In just two years of operation, L4R has secured 22 full-ride university scholarships and facilitated 9 internships for refugee youth. The iSpeakLead program now reaches 240 students across four refugee-affiliated schools. The IRIS Excellence Program recorded a 100% pass rate in Mathematics and French for its first cohort. And the 1500 Rwanda Book Project — a partnership with BelieveNBooks led by co-founder John Kelly Rukundo — is distributing 15,000 books across all refugee camps in Rwanda.

These aren't charity metrics. These are the results of a strategic effort to remove barriers so that brilliant young people can contribute to a better world.

"I believe that everyone, including a refugee, has potential yet to be unleashed." — John Kelly Rukundo, Co-Founder, L4R

What Cohort II Looks Like

The second Scholars Bootcamp takes place at Mahama & Kiziba Refugee Camps through July and August 2026. It is designed for Senior 6 students and recent graduates, with a focus on English proficiency, entrepreneurial thinking, leadership, and university application readiness.

Unlike traditional programs that prioritize perfect grades, L4R's selection criteria have always centered on resilience, commitment, and a drive to improve one's community. That philosophy remains unchanged — and it is why L4R scholars continue to outperform expectations in elite boarding schools and universities across the continent.

The Programs Behind the Numbers

  • iSpeakLead Program: Now serving 240 students across four refugee-affiliated schools, focused on public speaking, academic writing, and interview techniques.
  • Lighting Career Path Program: Connecting university scholars across East Africa to prestigious institutions and career opportunities — preventing graduates from returning to camp without viable employment.
  • IRIS Excellence Program (IEP): A targeted intervention helping scholars navigate elite boarding schools — 100% pass rate in Mathematics and French for Cohort 1.
  • 1500 Rwanda Book Project: 15,000 books being distributed across all refugee camps in Rwanda in partnership with BelieveNBooks.
  • Summer Scholars Bootcamp: Now entering its second cohort — the intensive residential program where it all began.

Built on Peer Accountability — Not Charity

As L4R co-founder Bernard Ndayishimiye puts it: "Not only us but also our young sisters and brothers. If you want to go far, you go with others." That spirit of collective advancement — built on peer accountability and mutual mentorship — is what distinguishes L4R from traditional aid programs.

The organization is supported by key partnerships with UNHCR Rwanda, Isomo Academy, and the Arizona State University Education4Humanity initiative. The third bootcamp is covered in Top Africa News — the first time L4R's Scholars Bootcamp has received external press coverage — a sign of a movement that can no longer be missed.

Cohort II: What the Second Bootcamp Built

Mahama Refugee Camp · Kirehe, Rwanda · 2026
"The first time, we were learning how to do this. The second time, we knew what we were doing — and it showed."

From Spark to System

The first Scholars Bootcamp in July 2025 was built on momentum and improvisation — 120 students showed up when 40 were expected, and the L4R team adapted in real time. Cohort II had the advantage of everything that moment taught us. We arrived with a clearer structure, stronger facilitators, and a deeper understanding of what refugee students actually need when they sit down in front of an opportunity.

The second cohort focused heavily on university application readiness — walking students through Common App, the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program, DAFI, and UCAS requirements with a level of specificity that Cohort I did not yet have. Students left not just inspired, but equipped with actual applications in progress and advisors to follow up with.

"Cohort II proved that the bootcamp wasn't a one-time miracle. It was a replicable model."

iSpeakLead Grows to 240 Students

Running alongside the bootcamp, the iSpeakLead program reached a milestone in 2026 — now operating across four refugee-affiliated schools and mentoring 240 students in public speaking, academic writing, and interview skills. The program is the feeder pipeline for the bootcamp, and the results are visible in how Cohort II students arrived: more articulate, more prepared, and more aware of their own potential.

19 Scholarships. 9 Internships. 100% at Elite Schools.

By the time Cohort III applications opened in June 2026, L4R's cumulative numbers since founding told a clear story: 19 full-ride university scholarships secured, 9 internships facilitated, and the IRIS Excellence Program's first cohort posting a 100% pass rate in Mathematics and French at elite boarding schools. Numbers that started as targets have become a track record.

100% Pass Rate: The IRIS Excellence Program's First Cohort

Rwanda · 2026
"We didn't just prepare them to survive elite schools. We prepared them to thrive in them."

The Gap Is Real — and Solvable

When a refugee student wins a place at one of Rwanda's elite boarding schools through the IRIS scholarship — that moment is a victory. But the classroom they walk into on Day 1 often represents a significant academic leap from the environment they came from. Camp-based schools are under-resourced. Curricula are disrupted. The expectations at national boarding schools are high.

The IRIS Excellence Program (IEP) was built specifically to address this transition — providing targeted academic support, mentorship, and structured intervention for IRIS scholars as they navigate the demands of Rwanda's most competitive secondary schools.

"100% pass rate in Mathematics and French for Cohort 1. Not because the bar was low. Because the preparation was high."

Cohort 1's results speak for themselves: a 100% pass rate in Mathematics and French. Every scholar who went through IEP cleared subjects that are historically among the most challenging for students transitioning from refugee camp schools. It is the clearest proof yet that the gap isn't about ability — it is about preparation, support, and the belief that these students can do it.

What Comes Next

With Cohort 1's results validated, IEP is expanding to support more scholars transitioning from IRIS into A'Level and eventually into university. The program now feeds directly into L4R's iLaunch program for A'Level students — creating a continuous support pathway from secondary school all the way through tertiary education and into employment or entrepreneurship.

A Day That Changed Everything

On Monday, July 21st, something extraordinary began at G.S. Paysannat L in Mahama Refugee Camp — the very school that once nurtured so many of the L4R founders — now the launchpad for the first-ever Light4Refugee Scholars Bootcamp.

More than 120 eager young minds showed up, nearly tripling the number expected. With no rigid structure and hearts pounding with nervous energy, the team adapted in real time. What unfolded was nothing short of remarkable.

"Students didn't just sit and listen. They leaned in. Asked hard questions. Some moments were loud with energy; others were quietly profound."

Sessions covered Critical Thinking, Leadership, Guided Reading, College Counseling, Career Exploration, and Mental Health & Psychosocial Support. Impact isn't about perfection — it's about presence. Day 1 proved that.
